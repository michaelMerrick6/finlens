# Sunday Brief
**September 13, 2026 · A weekly letter from Vail**

**Subject:** Sunday Brief: More sales, mixed activity, and the dates that matter

**Preview text:** 64 purchases, 97 sales, and a closer look at this week’s available congressional disclosures.

## A week of mixed moves

Good morning.

This week’s available disclosures included more sales than purchases—but that doesn’t give us a simple verdict on where Congress thinks the market is headed. Several politicians reported activity on both sides, and the transactions happened before the filings became public.

Here’s what stood out in Vail’s records.

## The week at a glance

**64 purchases · 97 sales · 6 politicians · 105 stocks and ETFs**

These counts cover eligible stock and ETF transactions disclosed September 7–13 in Vail’s current data. They include household disclosures and count transactions, not shares or dollars. The latest filing date in this snapshot is September 11.

## 1. Delaney’s disclosures included both buying and selling

April McClain Delaney accounted for 78 recorded transactions: 20 purchases and 58 sales. That was the largest transaction count among the six politicians represented in this week’s available records.

Liberty Formula One (FWONK), Life Time (LTH), and C.H. Robinson (CHRW) each appeared on both sides of her activity. The recorded purchases preceded an August 25 sale in each of those names.

The useful detail: seeing a stock on a purchase list doesn’t tell the whole story. Reading the accompanying sales and transaction dates gives a more complete picture of the disclosed activity—though still not a complete portfolio.

[Explore Delaney’s disclosures](https://www.vail.finance/politicians/M001232)

## 2. Cisneros led the purchase count

Gilbert Cisneros had 32 recorded purchases, half of the 64 purchases in this snapshot. His disclosures also included 23 sales, bringing his total to 55 transactions.

Together, Cisneros and Delaney accounted for 133 of the week’s 161 eligible transactions. That concentration matters: a busy week in the disclosure feed can reflect many entries from a small number of households, rather than widespread activity across Congress.

Purchase counts also don’t measure how much money someone invested. One large transaction can outweigh many smaller ones.

[Explore Cisneros’s disclosures](https://www.vail.finance/politicians/C001123)

## 3. No shared buying pattern in this snapshot

None of the stocks or ETFs in this seven-day dataset had purchases attributed to more than one distinct politician.

That is useful context, too. This week’s available records don’t support a story about multiple politicians converging on the same stock. A longer period could show a different pattern, and additional filings could change the picture.

## The date worth checking

A filing arriving this week can describe a transaction from weeks earlier. The earliest transaction in this snapshot occurred August 3. For example, Delaney’s September 9 filing included August activity.

Use the filing date to understand when information became public, and the transaction date to understand when the reported activity happened.
